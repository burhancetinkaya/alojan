# Free voice isolation: VoxLock trial — 2026-10-27

M3-02, M5-03, VXP-06; US-05/06; ADR 0003/0014. The owner requires a free
candidate, excluding paid/private SDK access. Krisp and LiveKit work is superseded.

## Decision

Do not enable automatic VoxLock isolation on the live line. It suppresses the
competing voice after a clean caller introduction, but selects the background
speaker when that speaker starts first. All four background-first cases reproduced
wrong-speaker selection and loss of the intended target in recognition. This is a
material failure for a caller who is silent while other people are talking.

This is a small controlled diagnostic, not a rejection of every possible use of
VoxLock. A reliable caller reference could change the outcome, but the current
telephone stream does not establish one. A louder/first voice is not caller identity.
No voice enrollment, stored voiceprint or production integration was introduced.

## Controlled cases

16 streams / 396.48 seconds: two target/background role assignments, four
conditions, and PCMA/PCMU. One public FLEURS Turkish read recording and one
synthetic EMA voice from the existing hash-checked manifest were used. The human
recording is `fleurs-1853-1`; the synthetic recording is `greeting`. They are
distinct voices, but this is not a representative or speaker-balanced corpus.
No real call audio was recorded, replayed or uploaded.

Conditions: target alone; background at one-quarter target peak; equal peaks;
and equal peaks with background starting five seconds before the target.
Peak matching is not RMS SIR calibration or a physical distance simulation.
The target utterance occurs twice with a seven-second gap. Background repeats
through the gap and second utterance; in the background-first case it also
overlaps the first utterance. Each stream starts with fresh automatic enrollment.
No future/loudest-window offline enrollment is supplied. Repeated target content
and an initially clean target in three conditions make these favorable diagnostics.

Paired bypass/filter recognition used the full second target utterance and a
five-second background-only window: **64 real CUDA decodes**. Background-only
decodes deliberately bypass VAD and can hallucinate, so emitted words cannot all
be counted as leaked background words. Output WAVs retain upstream streaming
alignment, including its end flush; buffering is measured separately. Seven
output samples exceeded normalized range and were retained in float WAVs/counts,
not silently clipped. Upstream flush produced 352 extra tail samples per stream;
the driver trims these to the original input duration.

## Results

Literal target-word error counts across both role assignments and both codecs;
each row has 54 reference words. Number/name/tokenization differences count.

| Condition | Bypass errors | VoxLock errors |
|---|---:|---:|
| Clean | 4 / 54 | 4 / 54 |
| Quieter background | 6 / 54 | 5 / 54 |
| Equal-peak background, target first | 50 / 54 | 6 / 54 |
| Background starts first | 48 / 54 | 54 / 54 |

In the eight target-first competing-voice cases, background-only output energy
was 33.6–44.3 dB below input. In the four background-first cases it was about
0.6 dB above input: the model kept that voice. Inspection of those transcriptions
confirmed the competing speaker's words replaced the intended target. Aggregate
improvement in the favorable cases does not meet the caller-preservation gate.

After suppression, direct Whisper decoding of background-only near-silence still
produced familiar subtitle/closing hallucinations. Noise filtering does not replace
speech/turn validation. Native VAD interruption counts were not measured here.

## Performance and checks

- Model/session construction: 1,076 ms, outside per-chunk timing.
- Per-case P95 push time at most 29.02 ms; worst push 63.49 ms including automatic
  speaker lock/warm-up. No observed push exceeded the 80 ms chunk interval.
- Total timed push/flush wall work was 0.272–0.317 times audio duration, with two
  threads. This is not CPU utilization, a concurrent-call capacity result or a
  hard deadline guarantee. Input chunks were fed unpaced.
- Automatic selection occurred 3.44–4.64 seconds into these streams. Before that,
  the upstream live mode passes audio through. This is an unprotected startup
  interval, not seconds of buffered phone audio.
- Maximum output deficit was 26 ms. Frame arrival/aggregation, worker scheduling
  and compute add latency; neither the 26 ms deficit nor the upstream 24 ms claim
  is measured end-to-end telephone delay.
- Codec/fixture self-checks, input/model checksums, finite/length checks, both
  complete evaluation scripts, Ruff lint/format and scoped diff whitespace checks
  passed. The Python 3.12 audioop deprecation warning remains.

No serving service/configuration/number binding changed. No native media integration,
handset acceptance, echo cancellation, quiet/distant caller, cancellation/concurrency
or soak qualification was performed after the wrong-speaker failure. Jev is unchanged.
Private raw results and downloaded models remain under `.local/voxlock-eval/`.
