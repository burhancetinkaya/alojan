# Native noise suppression before Whisper — 2026-10-24

M2-01 / M5-03; requirements §8.1, US-02/06; primary Linux evaluation under ADR
0003. Owner authorized researching established, popular open-source C++ options
and trying them before enabling anything. No live audio-path change was made.

## Decision

Keep denoising disabled in the carrier development gateway. WebRTC low is the
best candidate for further targeted noisy-speech work, but it does not preserve
quality across the tested conditions. RNNoise and Speex also fail that gate.
CPU processing is inexpensive; recognition quality, not speed, blocks promotion.
Do not add an unvalidated automatic noise classifier to mask this result.

## Protocol

Ubuntu, Intel i5-13600K CPU, RTX 4080 for the unchanged pinned faster-whisper
large-v3-turbo backend. Three suppression engines run on CPU through one C++
probe: WebRTC low/moderate, RNNoise and Speex -15 dB, plus bypass. Each processes
10 ms frames; RNNoise includes native 16→48→16 kHz resampling. No AEC, AGC,
extra VAD gate, hotwords, spelling replacements or second transcription pass.

The earlier public corpus manifest has 24 FLEURS Turkish read recordings and
16 synthetic EMA utterances. Five conditions give 200 speech inputs: PCMA,
10 dB white noise, fan-like noise, click-like noise and quiet speech. Add three
nonspeech controls, then run all five profiles: **1,015 decodes**. Noise is seeded
and injected before G.711 A-law conversion, not downloaded real-world noise.
There were 23 clipped input samples across the unique speech variants and zero
clipped denoiser output samples. All profiles receive identical input samples.

Fixture padding is 300 ms before and 200 ms after speech for causal filter startup
and tail retention. This is not a proposal to buffer the live call by 500 ms.
No offline delay compensation was used to improve the score. One processor is
initialized per utterance in this experiment; a future streaming integration
would require per-call state and separate interruption validation.

Whisper settings are the existing beam-5 Turkish profile. Profiles rotate order,
the model is warmed, and results are fully consumed. The human corpus is small,
read speech, previously reused and not verified speaker-balanced. Noise is
controlled synthetic perturbation; these are not handset or carrier recordings.

## Human speech recognition

Pooled literal word error rate; lower is better. Each cell has the same 24
recordings, 456 reference words. Number-format differences count as word edits.

| Condition | Bypass | WebRTC low | WebRTC moderate | RNNoise | Speex |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PCMA | 11.84% | 11.84% | 11.40% | 16.23% | 12.72% |
| White noise | 32.68% | 29.61% | 31.36% | 40.13% | 28.51% |
| Fan-like noise | 11.18% | 11.84% | 12.50% | 18.86% | 15.79% |
| Click-like noise | 11.84% | 11.84% | 11.84% | 16.67% | 15.35% |
| Quiet PCMA | 32.46% | 32.89% | 32.68% | 36.84% | 35.96% |

WebRTC low improved 29, tied 68 and worsened 23 of the 120 human condition pairs.
The unchanged PCMA aggregate hides individual regressions. The 23 worse inputs
were rerun twice for both bypass and WebRTC low (92 additional decodes): **all
46 paired comparisons reproduced worse word-edit counts**. This rules out a
one-off result on those selected cases, not all future stochastic variation.

Owner-requested inspection of five examples clarified an important scoring limit:
the click-noise case `fleurs-1724-4` changes “M.S. 400” to “milattan sonra dört yüz”.
That is a literal WER regression, not evidence of worse meaning. The 23/46 counts
must therefore remain word-edit counts, not counts of substantive comprehension
failures. Other reviewed cases do contain substantive damage: a short wrestling
sentence becomes largely unrelated, a geographical name/distance sentence loses
additional content, and quiet speech gains a nonexistent subtitle credit. The
promotion decision does not depend on treating formatting changes as meaning loss.

## Entities and nonspeech

WebRTC low retained the same curated product-label miss counts as bypass:
1/3 in PCMA, fan, clicks and quiet conditions; 2/3 in white noise. It did not fix
the noisy product-name errors. RNNoise added a product miss under fan-like noise;
Speex added one under quiet speech. FLEURS has no curated entity labels here.
Synthetic literal WER is affected strongly by digit-versus-word formatting and
must not be sold as an equivalent semantic error rate.

Manual inspection of the WebRTC-low synthetic name/date samples found the same
recognized content as bypass. The single synthetic phone example had an extra
digit in bypass; WebRTC low produced the intended digit sequence. This one
example is not a broad numeric-accuracy claim. No full numbers or private caller
transcripts are included in this report.

Exact digital silence stayed empty for every profile. Both forced noise-only
and hum-only controls still produced hallucinated stock phrases for every
profile. In particular, filtering did not eliminate the familiar “İzlediğiniz
için teşekkür ederim” failure mode. Existing speech/turn validation remains
necessary; these controls intentionally bypass the gateway VAD.

## Native cost and latency boundaries

Each profile processed about 1,862 seconds of audio, unpaced, on CPU. These are
diagnostic streaming-cost estimates, not whole-server utilization guarantees.

| Profile | Mean processing / 10 ms frame | Estimated one-core usage | Signal lag diagnostic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| WebRTC low | 0.0071 ms | 0.071% | 6 ms |
| WebRTC moderate | 0.0070 ms | 0.070% | 6 ms |
| RNNoise including resampling | 0.1034 ms | 1.034% | 25 ms |
| Speex | 0.0093 ms | 0.093% | 10 ms |

Lag was measured by cross-correlation on seeded broadband input; RNNoise's
resamplers account for 5 ms of its observed lag. This is separate from CPU work
and does not include jitter buffers, frame arrival, ASR or endpointing. Native
timing excludes construction, process startup and disk I/O. The worst per-file
P95 processing times were 0.0211 / 0.0208 / 0.2802 / 0.1482 ms respectively.
One WebRTC-low frame had a 1.356 ms wall-clock outlier; no hard sub-millisecond
deadline is claimed. Mean work per existing 20 ms packet would be twice the
10 ms mean, subject to live scheduling/cache effects.

Median whole-utterance ASR time was 127 ms bypass, 125 ms WebRTC low/moderate,
128 ms RNNoise and 126 ms Speex. These small differences are not a demonstrated
ASR speedup. All suppressors used CPU; an additional offline Whisper instance
used GPU memory. A mid-run whole-device observation was 8,963 MiB versus 6,624 MiB
beforehand; this was not continuous peak sampling or model-only accounting.

## Delivery and open work

Delivered isolated C++ probe, pinned build recipe, Python comparison and boundary
tests, with detailed results kept under ignored `.local/denoise-eval/`. Source
changes are on `codex/noise-suppression-evaluation`; no push or publication.
No gateway/ASR service restart, default change or native-media integration was
performed. Test/check outcomes are recorded in PLAN.md.

Further work, if pursued, needs representative consented noisy telephone speech
and a broader entity set. Test mild/adaptive suppression against that set before
changing the live path. Do not infer a reliable adaptive policy from this small
evaluation. Native streaming, barge-in, cancellation, PCMU and carrier listening
remain untested for denoising because no candidate passed the promotion gate.
